## Answers

Suggested solutions, not an official publisher key. Check explanations for ambiguity or inference.

### 1

Sample response: I consider two developers on my team successful. Both of them solve difficult problems, and each person helps others learn. Their success comes from persistence and clear communication.

Answers vary. Identify one or two people and explain your criteria for success.

### 2

Sample response: Yes. Both developers have a clear vision for their work. Neither gives up after a setback, and each enjoys solving problems.

Answers vary. Connect the chosen people to the three traits discussed in the source article.

### 3

get anywhere; get to the top

Get anywhere means achieve something or make progress. Get to the top means reach the highest level. Won't takes the base form.

### 4

get under way; get off to a good start

Get under way matches begin to happen. Get off to a good start describes a successful beginning. Get off the ground is also plausible for the first blank if the sprint is understood as a new initiative.

### 5

get ahead

Get ahead means make progress or become more successful.

### 6

gets; get in your way

It takes gets in the present simple. The plural subject setbacks takes get. The article's got to be cannot be inserted unchanged before frustrating.

### 7

get in their way; get on with

Let anything takes the base form get. Their refers to the developers. Get on with can be followed by an -ing form.

### 8

Sample response: I agree most with sentence 5. Setbacks are normal, so developers need to learn from them and get on with building.

Answers vary. Choose a sentence and support your opinion with a reason or example.

### 9

1–c; 2–d; 3–e; 4–b; 5–f; 6–a

The task distinguishes general beginnings, getting a project established, a successful beginning, progress, obstruction, and continuation.

### 10

All

Every takes a singular noun and verb; all takes a plural noun and verb here.

### 11

Both

Both refers to the two men together. Both the also produces a grammatical sentence with the same meaning.

### 12

Neither

Neither product means not one of the two products. It precedes a singular noun.

### 13

None of

None of precedes these developers and means not one member of that group.

### 14

No

No + singular noun can mean not a single member of a class.

### 15

b) All

All works before my + plural noun. Each would require a singular noun, or each of my close teammates is.

### 16

a) all of

All of can precede their efforts. Every requires a singular noun without their immediately after it.

### 17

b) No

No directly modifies the uncountable noun success. None cannot directly modify a noun.

### 18

a) None of

Use none of before an object pronoun such as them.

### 19

b) each

Each takes the singular noun person and emphasizes individual differences.

### 20

a) all of

All of fits before the + plural noun. Every requires a singular noun.

### 21

a) neither of

Before their + plural noun, use neither of. Neither alone would require a singular noun, as in neither project.

### 22

a) every

Every takes the singular noun developer and singular verb wants.

### 23

b) both of

Both of them refers to the two reviewers. Each would need of before them.

### 24

Sample practice approach: Take turns as A and B, using the completed answers. Then repeat the conversations with examples from your own experience.

This is fluency practice. Use the determiner choices from Exercise 6 and keep the noun forms appropriate.

### 25

Sample response: Yes, I know two developers I consider successful. Both of them build useful tools, and each developer spends time mentoring beginners.

Both of refers to two people; each takes singular developer.

### 26

Sample response: Each developer contributes in a different way. All of them communicate clearly, and no teammate is left without support.

Each emphasizes individuals, all of refers to the group, and no directly modifies teammate.

### 27

Sample response: All the successful developers I know keep learning. Every setback teaches them something, and none of them expects every project to be easy.

The sample limits its generalization to people the speaker knows. Every takes a singular noun; none of precedes them.

### 28

Sample response: I'm proud of two tools I built for my team. Both tools save time, and every teammate has used at least one of them.

Both modifies plural tools; every modifies singular teammate and takes has.

### 29

1. is; 2. are; 3. is

Each takes singular agreement, both takes plural agreement, and documentation is uncountable.

### 30

1. Every one of my teammates uses Git. / Each of my teammates uses Git.
2. Neither project was successful. / Neither of the projects was successful.
3. None of them gave up.

Use every one of rather than every of. Neither directly precedes a singular noun, while neither of requires a specified group or pronoun. Use none of before them.
