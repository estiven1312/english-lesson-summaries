# Gerunds and Infinitives

Sources: [[Sources and Reading Notes#S1 - Getting along|S1]], pp. 1-3; [[Sources and Reading Notes#S3 - Presenting a project update|S3]], p. 1; [[Sources and Reading Notes#S4 - Making time work|S4]], p. 1; [[Sources and Reading Notes#S5 - Why do we trust it so much|S5]], p. 1.

A verb's following form depends on the surrounding structure. Do not choose only by translating the meaning.

## The three choices

| Form | Examples | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base verb | work, join, finish | after core modals; after active *let/make + object* |
| To-infinitive | to work, to join, to finish | after *want*, *would like*, *enable + object* |
| -ing form | working, joining, finishing | after prepositions and certain verbs |

All examples below are new unless identified as source wording.

## After a preposition, use a noun or -ing

- I'm looking forward **to joining** the team.
- Instead **of arguing**, let's write an agreement.
- We began **by reviewing** the logs.
- They merged the code **without checking** it.
- Muting alerts prevents them **from interrupting** me.

**To** has two jobs:

- **I want to join.** → *to join* is an infinitive.
- **I look forward to joining.** → *to* is part of the expression *look forward to* and is a preposition.

## After certain verbs, use -ing

- **avoid taking on** too much work
- **put off discussing** the issue
- **give up checking** email at night
- **finish reviewing** the code

Learn the whole pattern. See [[Phrasal Verbs]] and [[Verb Complements and Cause and Result]].

## After certain verbs, use to + base verb

- **want to explain** the issue
- **would like to begin** the update
- **need to stop** now
- **enable someone to work** independently
- **cause a process to fail**

Source wording *I'd like to begin by showing you...* contains both patterns: **would like to begin** and **by showing**.

## Use the base form after modals

- We **should check** it.
- The service **might fail**.
- You **can join** us.

Related: [[Modal Verbs]]. Remember that *ought to*, *have to*, and *need to* include **to** in their own structures.

## An -ing form can be the subject

- **Working in short sprints** helps me concentrate.
- **Building a realistic plan** prevents overcommitment.

The whole activity is the subject, so it usually takes a singular verb: **Working ... helps**, not *help*.

## Common mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
| --- | --- |
| I look forward to join. | I look forward to joining. |
| Without check the output... | Without checking the output... |
| Let me to explain. | Let me explain. |
| This enables me focus. | This enables me to focus. |
| It prevents me from lose focus. | It prevents me from losing focus. |
