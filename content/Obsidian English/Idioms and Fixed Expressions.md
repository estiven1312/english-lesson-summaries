# Idioms and Fixed Expressions

Source: [[Sources and Reading Notes#S6 - Useful idioms for IELTS|S6]], selected expressions from pp. 5-20. This note selects useful grammar patterns rather than reproducing the full A-Z list. All sentence examples below are new.

An **idiom** has a conventional meaning that may not follow directly from its individual words. A **phrasal verb** describes a grammatical structure. An expression can be both: *give up* is a phrasal verb whose meaning often is not literal.

## What can change?

The verb and pronouns may change, but the fixed core usually remains:

- **I found my feet.** / **She found her feet.** / **They found their feet.**
- **He pulls his weight.** / **We pulled our weight.**
- **I keep an eye on it.** / **I kept an eye on it.**

Do not assume a fixed idiom allows the separability pattern of a phrasal verb:

- **Keep an eye on it.**
- Incorrect: *Keep it an eye on.*

Compare [[Phrasal Verbs]]: **Turn it off** has a different structure.

## Selected corrections to the PDF

The PDF contains some awkward labels and examples. Learn these standard forms:

| PDF wording | Form to learn |
| --- | --- |
| “No rocket science” | **It's not rocket science.** |
| “To split hair” | **split hairs** |
| “Driving up the wall” | **drive someone up the wall** |
| “as easy as a pie” in an example | **as easy as pie** |
| “Between the devil and deep sea” | **between the devil and the deep blue sea** |

For *catch someone's eye*, the meaning is **attract someone's attention**, which is broader than being beautiful. For *over the moon*, the useful meaning is **extremely happy**.

## Use idioms for a clear purpose

- In an informal update: **We're working against the clock.**
- In a more explicit update: **We have two hours left before the deadline.**

Choose expressions that fit the situation. The idioms PDF is supplementary vocabulary material; its exam-related claims have not been treated as current IELTS guidance.
