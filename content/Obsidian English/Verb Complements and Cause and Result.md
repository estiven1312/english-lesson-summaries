# Verb Complements and Cause and Result

Source: [[Sources and Reading Notes#S4 - Making time work|S4]], p. 1, article cards 1-8 and the start of the grammar chart.

To describe a cause or result, choose a verb **and the pattern that follows it**. Verbs with similar meanings can require different complements.

## Three patterns

Examples in this table are new, based on the lesson's patterns.

| Pattern | Verbs | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| verb + object + base verb | make, let, help | Interruptions **make me lose** focus. |
| verb + object + to-infinitive | cause, enable, help | Quiet mornings **enable us to concentrate**. |
| verb + object + from + -ing | prevent, stop, keep, protect | Time blocking **prevents messages from interrupting** me. |

**Help** permits either *help someone do* or *help someone to do*.

Source example: “Focused sprints can let your brain recover between tasks.”

## Make, let, help

- **make someone do** = cause the action, sometimes through force or pressure
- **let someone do** = allow the action
- **help someone (to) do** = make the action easier

New examples:

- The deadline **made us work** late.
- My manager **let me leave** early.
- A clear plan **helped me finish** the task.
- A clear plan **helped me to finish** the task.

Incorrect: *let me to leave*. Correct: **let me leave**.

## Cause and enable

- **cause something/someone to do** = bring about a result
- **enable someone/something to do** = make an action possible

New examples:

- Too many meetings **caused our focus time to disappear**.
- Better documentation **enabled Ana to work** independently.

Incorrect: *enabled Ana work*. Correct: **enabled Ana to work**.

## Prevent, stop, keep, protect

`verb + object + from + -ing`

New examples:

- Muting notifications **prevents messages from distracting** me.
- A shutdown routine **keeps work anxiety from following** me home.
- A calendar buffer **stops stress from building up**.
- Async communication **protects us from wasting** time.

Use **-ing** because *from* is a preposition. See [[Gerunds and Infinitives]]. For these study notes, keep *from* in the prevention pattern, even though it can be omitted after *stop* in some usage.

Compare:

- **I stopped checking messages.** = I no longer checked them.
- **I stopped messages from interrupting me.** = I prevented an interruption.

## One idea, different structures

New examples:

- Interruptions **make developers lose** focus.
- Interruptions **cause developers to lose** focus.
- Time blocking **helps developers keep** their focus.
- Time blocking **prevents interruptions from breaking** their focus.

## Combine with passive reporting

**Time blocking is said to help developers focus.**

- *is said to* reports a claim: [[Reporting Passive]].
- *help developers focus* expresses the claimed benefit.
