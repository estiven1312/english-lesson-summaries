### B

1. If you can't finish a task, **hand it off** to a teammate before the sprint ends.
2. Instead of **putting up with** bad communication habits, figure out some agreements.
3. Don't **put off** talking about it when a problem **comes up**.
4. You might find out you have to **give up** a few bad habits of your own.

### C

1. Are you **looking forward to joining** a new team this year?
2. Have you **come up with any team agreements** in your current role?
3. Do you always **close out tickets** right away when they're done? / Do you always **close tickets out** right away when they're done?
4. What problems **come up** in your team?
5. Do things usually **work out**?
6. Has a teammate ever **handed off a task** late or without context? / Has a teammate ever **handed a task off** late or without context?
7. If you had a problem with a teammate's habits, would you **put up with it**?
8. Or would you **go over things** and find a solution?
9. Do you ever **pick up unfinished tasks** if you **run out of time**? / Do you ever **pick unfinished tasks up** if you **run out of time**?

C9 follows the source wording, although the situation is unusual: people normally take on extra tasks when they **have** time. For a more natural question, replace the condition with *if you have spare time*.

### D

1. **A - them off.** Pronouns go between a separable verb and its particle.
2. **B - go over it.** The review/discuss meaning has fixed object position.
3. **B - meeting.** *To* is a preposition in *look forward to*.
4. **A - came up with.** Keep the expression together.

### E - sample answers

1. If a problem comes up, tell the team.
2. Hand off unfinished tasks with clear notes.
3. Go over the acceptance criteria before starting.
4. Turn off unnecessary notifications during focus time.
5. Don't put off difficult feedback.
6. Close out tickets when the work is complete.

Other sensible agreements are valid. Review [[Phrasal Verbs]] and [[Team Agreements and If Clauses]].
